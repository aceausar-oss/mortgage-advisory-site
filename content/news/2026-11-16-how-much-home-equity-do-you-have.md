## How much equity do homeowners have right now?

More than ever. Americans with a mortgage held **$17.5 trillion** in home equity in the third quarter, a record and up **1.4%** from a year earlier. The average borrower could tap about **$204,000** while still keeping 20% equity in the home, up **$16,600** from a year ago.

California stands out. Of the **2.4 million** mortgage holders with at least $1 million in equity, **49%** live here. If you've owned in Southern California for a while, there's a good chance you have more equity than you think.

## How are prices doing where we lend?

Prices are still rising in most of Southern California, and cooling in parts of Texas, Florida and Colorado.

| Area | Median single-family price, September | Change in a year |
|---|---|---|
| California (statewide) | $899,720 | +1.7% |
| Orange County | $1,480,000 | +5.6% |
| San Diego County | $1,050,000 | +6.1% |
| Los Angeles County | $994,670 | +1.2% |

The Case-Shiller index, which tracks the same homes selling again, showed U.S. prices up **2.1%** in a year through August. Miami was up **4.2%**, while Tampa (**-0.4%**), Denver (**-0.8%**) and Dallas (**-0.8%**) slipped a little. Small dips like these mostly mean buyers have more room to negotiate. They don't wipe out the equity owners have built over the years.

## What is Prop 37, and who can use it?

California voters passed **Prop 37** on November 3. It creates a state down payment program, run by the California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA), for middle-income buyers of **newly built homes**. Here's how it's set up:

- **Who:** California residents for at least one year, earning less than **twice the area median income**, who will live in the home.
- **What homes:** New construction (or the first sale of a building converted to homes), bought by the first owner, under a price cap of roughly $1 million to $1.5 million depending on the county.
- **How much:** Up to **17%** of the price, paid back through the buyer's mortgage. The buyer puts at least **3%** down.
- **When:** Not set yet. CalHFA still has to build the program, so it isn't available today.

It's good timing on the new-home side. The median new home in the U.S. sold for **$400,400** in September, down **3.9%** in a year, and builders had **8.4 months** of homes for sale. With that many homes to sell, builders may have more room to negotiate.
