## Are home prices still holding up?

Yes. Fewer people bought homes in October, but prices didn't fall. Sales dropped **5.5%** from September to a yearly pace of **3.75 million**, the slowest since 2010. Even so, the typical home sold for **$416,700**, up **0.4%** from a year earlier. That's the 40th month in a row that prices were higher than the year before.

Buyers also have more to choose from. There were **5.1 months** of homes for sale, up from 4.4 a year ago, and homes sat a median of **35 days** before selling. A lot of buyers stepped back after borrowing costs jumped in late September. The ones still shopping have more room to negotiate.

## How are prices doing where we lend?

In California, sales fell **12.7%** from a year ago, but the statewide median of **$886,760** was about where it was last October. Homes still sold fast, in a median of **29 days**, at **98.5%** of the asking price.

Southern California is a split picture:

| County | Median price, October 2026 | Change in a year |
|---|---|---|
| Orange | $1,500,000 | +7.8% |
| San Diego | $1,037,000 | +5.3% |
| Ventura | $900,000 | −5.0% |
| Los Angeles | $955,090 | −0.6% |
| Riverside | $610,000 | −3.9% |
| San Bernardino | $501,510 | +1.9% |

The coast keeps climbing, while the Inland Empire and Ventura are giving back a little. Across the South, which includes Texas and Florida, the median was **$360,900**, down **0.4%**. In the West, which includes Colorado, it was **$624,300**, flat with a year ago.

## Why is home equity at a record if sales are slow?

Because most owners aren't selling. U.S. mortgage holders now have a record **$17.5 trillion** in equity. The average mortgage holder could borrow about **$204,000** and still keep 20% equity in the home, up **$16,600** from a year ago.

And **2.4 million** mortgage holders now have at least **$1 million** in equity. California has **49%** of them. If you bought in Orange County or San Diego 15 or 20 years ago, there's a good chance you're one of them.

New homes are a different story. The typical new home sold for **$400,400** in September, down **3.9%** from a year earlier, with **8.4 months** of new homes on the market. Builders have more homes than buyers right now.

## What does this mean for you?

**If you own your home:** Your equity is likely higher than you think, even with flat prices. You don't have to sell or give up a low first-mortgage rate to use it. Compare a [HELOC vs. a cash-out refinance](/answers/heloc-vs-cash-out-refinance), and if you're carrying credit cards or car loans too, check your [Life Rate](/life-rate) to see what all your debt really costs you.

**If you're buying:** Slower sales and more listings mean you can ask for repairs, credits, or a better price, and builders are motivated too. You also don't need 20% down. See [how much you really need to put down](/answers/how-much-down-payment-do-i-need) and how a [purchase loan](/loans/purchase) works with us.

DSCR loans qualify on the rent, and most lenders don't cap how many you own. ## Our take

When sales drop like this, I get calls asking if prices are about to crash. I don't see it. People aren't selling because they don't have to, and that keeps prices steady. Here in Orange County, prices are still up almost 8% in a year. What I do see is a lot of homeowners sitting on six or seven figures of equity and paying high interest on credit cards at the same time. That's the thing I'd fix first.
