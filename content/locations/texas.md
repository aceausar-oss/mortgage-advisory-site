## What's different about a mortgage in Texas?

Texas protects homesteads more than almost any state, so home equity loans follow special rules:

- **80% limit:** when you take cash out of your homestead, all your loans combined generally can't exceed 80% of the home's value.
- **12-day waiting period:** you'll get a Texas home equity notice, and you can't close until at least 12 days after.
- **Once a year:** generally only one Texas home equity loan (cash-out) every 12 months.
- **Fee cap:** certain lender and broker fees on Texas home equity loans are capped at 2% of the loan.
- **Where you close:** Texas home equity loans close at a lender's, title company's, or attorney's office, not at your kitchen table.

Other Texas basics:

- **No state income tax, but higher property taxes** than most states. Filing your **homestead exemption** as soon as you move in can lower them.
- **Title insurance rates are set by the state,** so they don't vary from company to company.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A homeowner in Plano owns a $500,000 home and owes $220,000. Under the 80% rule, total borrowing can go up to $400,000, so there's room for up to $180,000 in cash. She takes $60,000 to pay off credit cards and finish her backyard, waits out the 12-day notice, and closes at a local title company.
