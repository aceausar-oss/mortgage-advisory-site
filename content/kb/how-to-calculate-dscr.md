## What's the formula?

**DSCR = monthly rent ÷ monthly payment (PITIA)**

PITIA means **P**rincipal, **I**nterest, **T**axes, **I**nsurance, and **A**ssociation (HOA) dues.

## Can you show me an example?

| | Property A | Property B |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly rent | $3,000 | $2,400 |
| Principal & interest | $2,050 | $2,000 |
| Taxes | $350 | $400 |
| Insurance | $150 | $200 |
| HOA | $0 | $150 |
| **Total payment** | **$2,550** | **$2,750** |
| **DSCR** | **1.18** | **0.87** |

Property A pays for itself with room to spare. Property B falls short; it may still work on a program that allows a ratio under 1.0, usually with a bigger down payment.

## Where does the rent number come from?

- **On a purchase:** usually the appraiser's market rent estimate, or the existing lease if it's already rented
- **On a refinance:** the current lease, sometimes with proof the rent is being paid
- **Short-term rentals:** past booking history or a short-term rental market report, depending on the program

## How can I improve the ratio?

- **Put more down** to shrink the payment
- **Shop the insurance**; it's often the easiest number to move
- **Buy down the rate** with points
- **Choose an interest-only option**, if the program offers one, to lower the early payment

## Our take

Investors get surprised by insurance and property taxes more than anything else. Before you make an offer, get a real insurance quote and check the tax rate; I'll plug both into the ratio so there are no surprises in underwriting.
