## Why does it cost more?

Bank statement loans are **Non-QM** loans. They can't be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so lenders take on more risk and price for it. The cost shows up in:

- **The rate:** higher than a comparable conventional loan
- **The down payment:** often 10% to 20% or more, depending on credit and loan size
- **Reserves:** some programs want several months of payments in savings

## What affects my price?

- **Credit score:** higher scores get meaningfully better pricing
- **Down payment:** more down usually means a lower rate
- **12 vs. 24 months of statements:** some programs price them differently
- **Loan size and property type**

## When is it worth it?

- Your **tax returns show much less** than you actually earn because of write-offs.
- The **right home is available now**, and waiting a year or two for your returns to catch up would cost you more.
- You have a **plan to refinance** into a conventional loan once your returns support it.

It's **not** worth it if a conventional loan would work. I'll always check that first.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A salon owner in Sacramento deposits about $15,000 a month, but her tax returns show about $45,000 a year after write-offs. A conventional loan won't get her the home she needs; a 24-month bank statement loan with 15% down does. We plan to revisit a conventional refinance after she files two more years of returns.
