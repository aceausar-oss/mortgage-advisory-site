## How are they different?

| | Home equity loan | Traditional HELOC |
|---|---|---|
| How you get money | One lump sum at closing | A credit line; draw as needed |
| Rate | Fixed | Usually variable |
| Payment | Fixed principal and interest | Often interest-only at first, then jumps |
| Borrow again? | No | Yes, during the draw period |
| Best for | One known expense | Ongoing or uncertain costs |

Both are **second mortgages**: they sit behind your first mortgage, and your first mortgage's rate and payment stay the same.

## Where do the HELOCs you arrange fit?

In between, with the best of both:

- **Fixed or variable rate:** your choice.
- **Principal and interest from day one:** no interest-only period and no payment jump later.
- **Flexibility:** depending on the program, you may be able to draw more as you pay down the balance.

More on how they work: [How does a HELOC work?](/answers/how-does-a-heloc-work)

## Which one should I choose?

- **One project with a set price** (a roof, a kitchen, paying off specific debts): a home equity loan or a fixed-rate HELOC.
- **Costs spread over time** (a remodel in phases, tuition each semester): a HELOC.
- **Want a backup for emergencies:** a HELOC, if the program lets you keep the line open without drawing it all.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A homeowner in Pueblo needs exactly $45,000 for a new roof and HVAC system. He takes a fixed-rate option with one lump sum and a set payoff date. His neighbor, planning a remodel in three phases over a year, chooses a HELOC so she only borrows (and pays interest on) each phase as the bills come in.
