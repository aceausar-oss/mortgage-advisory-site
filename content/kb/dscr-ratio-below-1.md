## What are the options when the ratio is under 1.0?

- **Lower-ratio programs:** some accept a ratio around 0.75 to 0.99, usually with 25% to 30% down and strong credit
- **No-ratio programs:** a few skip the rent test, typically with the largest down payments and higher rates
- **Change the numbers:** more down, a rate buy-down, cheaper insurance, or interest-only can push the ratio over 1.0

## Why would anyone buy a property that doesn't cash-flow?

Some investors buy in high-appreciation areas, plan to raise rents, or are renovating to increase the rent. That can work, but you're covering the shortfall every month out of pocket, so have a plan and reserves.

## Example (illustrative)

An investor in Irvine finds a condo with $3,100 rent and a $3,500 payment, a ratio of 0.89. A lower-ratio program works with 30% down. He also shops the insurance and buys down the rate, which brings the payment to $3,250 and the ratio to 0.95. He knows he'll cover about $150 a month until the rent rises.

## Our take

I'll tell you straight when a property doesn't cash-flow. Sometimes the right move is a bigger down payment; sometimes it's a different property. Either way, you'll see the monthly shortfall in real dollars before you commit.
