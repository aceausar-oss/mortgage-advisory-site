## Why do California investors buy out of state?

Many California investors find that homes in **Texas, Florida, and Colorado** cost less relative to rent, so the DSCR ratio works more easily. Each market has its own trade-offs: property taxes, insurance costs, and landlord rules all differ.

## What changes when the property is in another state?

- **Property taxes:** Texas has no state income tax but higher property tax rates in many areas, which raise the payment
- **Insurance:** Florida and parts of Texas can have high insurance costs from storms; get quotes before you offer
- **Closing:** you can usually sign remotely or with a mobile notary, depending on the title company and the state
- **Property management:** budget for a local manager if you won't be nearby

## Do I need to be licensed or live there?

No. **You** don't need to live in or be licensed in that state. The **lender** needs to be licensed where the property is, which is why it matters that we're licensed in all four states.

## Example (illustrative)

An investor in Orange County buys a $310,000 rental near Dallas. The rent is $2,350; with Texas property taxes, the full payment comes to $2,150, a ratio of 1.09. She closes with 25% down, signs with a mobile notary in Irvine, and hires a local property manager.
