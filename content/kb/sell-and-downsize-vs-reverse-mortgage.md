## How do the three paths compare?

| | Sell and downsize | Stay with a reverse mortgage | Reverse mortgage for purchase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where you live | A smaller home, or renting | Your current home | A new home that fits better |
| Cash freed up | Often the most | Depends on age and home value | Whatever you don't put into the new home |
| Monthly mortgage payment | None if you pay cash | None required | None required |
| Upfront costs | Selling costs, often 6% to 8%, plus moving | Loan costs, often paid from the loan | Loan costs plus buying costs |
| Taxes | Gain above $250,000 (single) or $500,000 (married) can be taxable | No tax on loan money | Selling your old home can still trigger tax |
| Equity for heirs | Cash or the new home | Whatever's left after the loan is repaid | Whatever's left after the loan is repaid |

## When does downsizing make the most sense?

- The house is **more than you need**: stairs, a big yard, empty bedrooms
- **Upkeep, taxes, or insurance** are becoming too much
- You want to **move closer to family**, or somewhere easier to age in place
- You'd like **cash in the bank** more than equity in a house

## When does staying put make sense?

- You **love your home and neighborhood**, and it works for you as you age
- Selling would mean a **large taxable gain** (common for long-time California owners). See [reverse mortgages and taxes](/answers/reverse-mortgage-taxes-retirement)
- A smaller home nearby **wouldn't cost much less**

## What is a reverse mortgage for purchase?

A HECM for Purchase lets you **buy your next home with a reverse mortgage**. You make a large down payment, often from selling your current home, and the reverse mortgage covers the rest. After closing, there's **no monthly mortgage payment**. You still pay property taxes, insurance, and upkeep. The down payment depends mostly on your age: the older you are, the less you need to put down.

It's a way to right-size **without** spending all your equity on the new home, and without taking on a new payment.

## Are there tax breaks for moving?

- **California:** under Prop 19, homeowners 55 and older can generally move their property tax base to a replacement home anywhere in California, up to three times.
- **Florida:** "Save Our Homes" portability can let you carry part of your homestead tax savings to a new Florida home.

Rules have limits and deadlines, so confirm with your county assessor.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A couple in their early 70s in Thousand Oaks own a two-story home worth $1.3 million. The stairs are getting hard. They sell, use part of the proceeds as a down payment on a $750,000 single-story home, and a reverse mortgage for purchase covers the rest. They move in with no mortgage payment and several hundred thousand dollars in the bank.

## Our take

There's no wrong answer here, only the one that fits the next 20 years of your life. I'll put all three side by side: what you'd walk away with, what you'd pay each month, and what's left for your family.
