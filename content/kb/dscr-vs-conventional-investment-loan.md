## How do they compare?

| | Conventional investment loan | DSCR loan |
|---|---|---|
| **Qualify on** | Your personal income and debt-to-income | The property's rent |
| **Documents** | Tax returns, W-2s or business returns | Lease or rent estimate; no tax returns |
| **Rate** | Usually lower | Usually higher |
| **Down payment** | About 15% to 25% | About 20% to 25% |
| **Property limit** | Most investors capped at 10 financed properties | No set limit at most lenders |
| **LLC ownership** | Generally not at closing | Usually allowed |
| **Prepayment penalty** | None | Common |

## When is conventional better?

- Your income is easy to document and your **debt-to-income has room**
- You want the **lowest rate** and no prepayment penalty
- You have fewer than 10 financed properties

## When is DSCR better?

- Your tax returns **understate your income** (lots of write-offs)
- Your **debt-to-income is maxed out** from other properties
- You've hit or are near the **10-property limit**
- You want to **close in an LLC**
- You want a faster, simpler file with less paperwork

## Our take

Many of my investors use both: conventional loans for their first few rentals while their debt-to-income allows, then DSCR as the portfolio grows. I'll run both on your next property and show you the real difference in rate and payment.
