## What's the quick math?

**Home value × 80% − mortgage balance = estimated HELOC amount**

- $600,000 × 80% = $480,000
- $480,000 − $300,000 owed = **about $180,000**

Try your own numbers on the [equity slider](/#equity-heading), or see [how we estimate](/how-we-estimate).

## What changes the real number?

- **Appraised value:** the home's value as it is today, often from a digital valuation.
- **Credit score:** stronger credit can unlock a higher combined limit on some programs.
- **Debt-to-income:** the new payment has to fit your budget. See [HELOC credit and DTI requirements](/answers/heloc-credit-score-dti).
- **Property type:** second homes and rentals usually get lower limits than your primary home.
- **Program minimums and maximums:** every program has a smallest and largest line.

## Should I borrow the maximum?

Usually not. Borrow what you need plus a sensible cushion. A smaller line means a smaller payment and leaves equity as a buffer if home values dip.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A homeowner in Tampa's home is worth $450,000 with $250,000 owed. The 80% math gives up to $110,000. She needs $50,000 for a pool and a car payoff, so she borrows $55,000 to leave a cushion, keeping her payment comfortable.
