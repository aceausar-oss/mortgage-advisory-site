## Why did this happen?

Your mortgage payment usually includes **property taxes and homeowners insurance**, collected into an escrow account. Once a year, your servicer runs an **escrow analysis**. If your tax bill or insurance premium rose, the account paid out more than it collected, and that gap is the **shortage**.

## Why did my payment go up so much?

Two things happen at once:

1. **Catching up:** the shortage is usually spread over the next 12 payments.
2. **Going forward:** your monthly escrow amount rises to match the new, higher bills.

That's why the jump can feel bigger than the shortage itself.

## What can I do about it?

- **Pay the shortage in one lump sum,** if you can. That removes the catch-up part of the increase.
- **Shop your homeowners insurance.** Premiums have jumped in many areas; a new quote can save real money.
- **Check your property tax bill and exemptions,** like a homestead exemption, and appeal your assessed value if it looks too high.
- **Read the escrow statement** carefully; mistakes happen, and you can ask the servicer to explain or correct it.

## Can I get rid of escrow?

Sometimes. Some loans let you pay taxes and insurance yourself once you have enough equity, often for a small fee or pricing adjustment. FHA and VA loans generally require escrow.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A homeowner in Tampa gets a letter showing a $1,450 shortage after her insurance premium rose. Her payment goes up by the shortage spread over 12 months plus the higher monthly insurance cost. She shops her policy, finds a lower premium, and pays the shortage in one lump sum, bringing her payment most of the way back down.
