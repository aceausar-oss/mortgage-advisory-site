## What are the three objections I'll hear most?

| What the client says | What's actually true |
|---|---|
| **"It sounds too good to be true, so it must be a scam."** | There's no required monthly mortgage payment, and the trade-off is that the balance grows over time. It's a heavily regulated loan. A HECM is FHA-insured, and every HECM borrower meets with an independent HUD-approved counselor before applying. |
| **"Only desperate people get one."** | For most retirees, the home is the biggest asset they own. Using it as part of a plan is a choice, not a last resort. More and more of these borrowers work closely with a financial professional, like you. |
| **"The bank ends up with the house."** | The lender holds a lien, like any mortgage. The client keeps title and can stay as long as they live there and keep up with taxes, insurance, and upkeep. Heirs can keep the home by paying off the loan (on a HECM, the lesser of the balance or 95% of the appraised value), or sell it and keep what's left. On a HECM, no one ever owes more than the home is worth. |

## What if the facts don't settle it?

Ask one open question and listen: **"What's the number one thing holding you back?"** The answer is usually specific: the kids' inheritance, a story a friend told, the costs, or simply not wanting a loan at their age. Once you know the real concern, you can answer it directly, or agree that a reverse mortgage isn't the right fit. See [when a reverse mortgage is a bad idea](/answers/when-is-a-reverse-mortgage-a-bad-idea).

## How do I introduce it in 30 seconds?

Keep it to four parts: **who you are, what's possible, how it helps them, and a next step.** For example:

> "As part of your plan, I look at every resource you have, including your home. Today's reverse mortgages are a lot more flexible than most people think. They can add cash flow, cover a big goal, or protect your investments in a down market. Would you like a short overview to read before our next meeting?"

The goal isn't a decision. It's permission to keep talking.

## Why does the family talk matter so much?

Many clients assume their children want the house. Often the children care more about their parents being comfortable and secure, and they may not want the home at all. When the two sides never talk, clients hold back from a plan that would help everyone. Encourage clients to include their children, or anyone else who'd help decide, in the conversation early. We're happy to explain the options to the whole family at once.

## What should clients be wary of in reverse mortgage ads?

The reverse mortgage industry's own ethics guidelines flag these as red flags. If a client mentions an ad like this, slow down:

- Calling a HECM a **"government benefit"** or saying a lender is **"HUD-approved"** or endorsed by a seniors' group
- **"No cost," "no fees," "no risk,"** or **"no payments"** without explaining what the borrower still owes and pays
- **"Pre-approved"** or **"pre-qualified"** without saying what approval actually requires
- **"Respond now or lose your benefit,"** or other pressure to act fast
- Saying the client must **also buy an annuity, insurance, or investment** to get the loan
- **Fake checks or currency** in the mail
- **Celebrity endorsements** that don't say they're paid
- Big promises like **"we pay off your mortgage"** with no mention of the trade-offs

## Our take

You don't need to be a reverse mortgage expert to start the conversation. Answer the myths with facts, ask what's really holding the client back, and bring the family in. Then let us handle the numbers, and tell your client honestly if it isn't a fit.
