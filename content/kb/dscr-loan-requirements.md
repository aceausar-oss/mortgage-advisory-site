## What do DSCR lenders actually check?

Instead of your job and tax returns, a DSCR loan looks at the property and your track record with money:

| What we check | What most programs look for |
|---|---|
| **The ratio (DSCR)** | Rent at or above the full monthly payment (a ratio of 1.0 or higher). Some programs go lower with more down. |
| **Down payment** | About 20% to 25% on a purchase |
| **Credit score** | Many programs start around 660; better pricing usually starts in the 700s |
| **Reserves** | Usually 3 to 12 months of the property's payment in the bank after closing |
| **Property** | A rental: single-family, 2 to 4 units, condos, and townhomes |

## What don't you need?

- **No personal tax returns, W-2s, or pay stubs**
- **No debt-to-income ratio** on your personal income
- **No employment verification**

That's why DSCR loans work so well for self-employed investors and people with a lot of write-offs.

## What can't a DSCR loan be used for?

It's for **investment properties only**. You can't use it for the home you live in, a second home you use yourself, or a property you're flipping in a few months.

## Example (illustrative)

A nurse in Riverside owns one rental and wants a second. Her income is fine, but her debt-to-income is maxed out from her first rental and her own home. On a DSCR loan, the new property's $2,400 rent covers its $2,250 payment, she has 25% down and six months of reserves, and her personal debt-to-income never comes into it.
