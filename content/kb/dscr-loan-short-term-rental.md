## How is short-term rental income counted?

Depending on the program, the lender uses:

- **Your booking history** (often 12 months of statements from Airbnb, VRBO, or your property manager), or
- **A short-term rental market report** estimating what the property should earn, for a property that isn't rented yet

Some programs reduce the income for expenses like cleaning and platform fees, which lowers the ratio.

## What's different from a long-term rental loan?

- **Bigger down payment:** often about 25%
- **Slightly higher rate** in many programs
- **More reserves** may be required
- **Local rules matter:** if the city or HOA bans or limits short-term rentals, the loan may not work

## Which cities have short-term rental rules?

Many cities and HOAs in California, Florida, Texas, and Colorado limit short-term rentals with permits, caps, or outright bans. Rules change often, so check the current rules for the exact address and the HOA's documents before you buy.

## Example (illustrative)

An investor buys a two-bedroom near the beach in Florida for $500,000. The market report estimates $4,800 a month in bookings; after the program's expense reduction, the ratio comes in at 1.1. With 25% down she qualifies, and her property manager's 12 months of statements will help when she refinances later.
