## How do they compare?

| | Personal loan | HELOC / home equity loan |
|---|---|---|
| Secured by | Nothing (unsecured) | Your home |
| Typical rate | Higher; depends heavily on credit | Usually lower |
| Term | Often 2 to 7 years | Often 5 to 30 years |
| Payment | Higher, pays off faster | Lower, can stretch longer |
| Closing costs | Often an origination fee | Varies by program |
| Speed | Very fast | Days to a few weeks |

## When is a personal loan better?

- **Smaller balances**, where closing costs on a home loan would eat the savings
- **Strong credit**, which gets a personal loan rate closer to home equity rates
- You **don't want your home involved** at all
- You want a **short, forced payoff**

## When is home equity better?

- **Larger balances**, where the rate difference saves real money
- You want a **lower monthly payment** to free up cash flow
- You have **plenty of equity** and a stable income

## Whichever you choose

Compare the **total cost**, not just the payment, and pick a payoff date you'll actually stick to. See [the risks of turning card debt into home debt](/answers/risks-of-secured-debt-consolidation).

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A homeowner in Denver owes $9,000 on one card and has excellent credit. A 3-year personal loan pays it off fast without touching his home, so that's his pick. His sister owes $46,000 across six cards; a fixed-rate HELOC saves her far more in interest and cuts her monthly payments in half.
