## Who is eligible?

In general, with a discharge that isn't dishonorable:

| Who | Minimum service |
|---|---|
| Currently serving | 90 continuous days of active duty |
| Veterans who served since August 2, 1990 | 24 continuous months, or the full period you were called to active duty (at least 90 days) |
| Veterans who served 1980 to 1990 | 24 continuous months, or the full period called up (at least 181 days) |
| National Guard and Reserve | 6 creditable years, or 90 days of non-training active duty (Title 10 for the Guard) |
| Surviving spouses | Some qualify, for example those receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation |

There are exceptions for service members discharged early for a service-connected disability or certain other reasons. Earlier service eras have their own rules.

## What is a COE?

Your **Certificate of Eligibility** shows the lender that you qualify for a VA loan and how much **entitlement** you have. It isn't an approval for the loan itself; the lender still reviews your credit and income.

## How do I get my COE?

1. **Ask your lender.** We can usually pull it online through VA's system in minutes.
2. **Request it yourself** on VA.gov.
3. **Mail VA Form 26-1880** if you can't use the online options.

If VA needs more records, like your DD214 or a statement of service, the lender will tell you exactly what to send.

## What if I've used my VA loan before?

You may still have **remaining entitlement**, or you can **restore** it by selling the home and paying off the loan. Your COE shows what's available. Many veterans use the benefit more than once.
