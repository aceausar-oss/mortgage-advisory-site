## What is a prepayment penalty?

It's a fee you pay if you **pay off the loan early**, usually by selling or refinancing. It doesn't apply to your normal monthly payments, and many programs let you pay down some extra principal each year without a penalty.

## What do the common structures look like?

| Structure | How it works |
|---|---|
| **5-4-3-2-1** | 5% of the balance if paid off in year 1, 4% in year 2, and so on down to 1% in year 5 |
| **3-2-1** | 3%, 2%, then 1% over the first three years |
| **Fixed** | The same percentage (for example 3%) for a set number of years |
| **None** | No penalty, usually at a higher rate |

Prepayment penalties are allowed on investment property loans in most places, but some states limit them. We'll confirm what applies to your property.

## How much could it cost me?

On a $300,000 balance, a 3% penalty is **$9,000**. That's why your plan matters: if you might sell or refinance within two years, a shorter penalty or no penalty may save you money even at a slightly higher rate.

## How do I choose?

- **Holding 5+ years?** A longer penalty for the lower rate often makes sense.
- **Might refinance when rates drop?** Choose a shorter penalty.
- **Planning to sell or flip soon?** Look at no-penalty options, or a different loan type altogether.
