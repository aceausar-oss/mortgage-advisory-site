## Does it affect Social Security or Medicare?

**No.** Social Security retirement benefits and Medicare aren't based on your income or assets, and reverse mortgage money is a loan, not income. Your checks and your coverage stay the same.

## What about Medicaid and SSI?

These are **needs-based**, so they look at your income and assets:

- **In the month you receive it,** reverse mortgage money generally isn't counted as income.
- **If you keep it into the next month,** it can count as an asset, which could affect eligibility.

## How do I avoid a problem?

- **Take a line of credit** and draw only what you'll spend that month. Money left in the line isn't in your bank account, so it isn't counted as your asset.
- **Avoid a large lump sum** sitting in savings if you're on or near Medicaid or SSI.
- **Talk to a benefits specialist or elder law attorney** before closing if you receive Medicaid or SSI. Rules vary by state, and I don't give legal advice.

## Example scenario (illustrative)

A 76-year-old homeowner in Hialeah receives SSI and Medicaid. Instead of taking a lump sum, she sets up a HECM line of credit and draws just enough each month to cover home repairs and utilities. Because she spends each draw in the month she takes it, her benefits aren't affected.

## Our take

For most retirees, a reverse mortgage doesn't touch Social Security or Medicare at all. If you're on Medicaid or SSI, the structure matters, and a line of credit is usually the answer. Let's plan it together with your benefits advisor.
